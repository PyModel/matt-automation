# Run a VECTOR session at work

Use this guide to help colleagues turn one familiar task into a practical way of working with AI. Leave with a clear result to aim for, a few steps to try, and a person responsible for learning from the trial. No technical background or new software is required; a conversation and a shared document or sheet of paper are enough.

## Before the session

Invite the person who does the work, someone to guide the conversation, and the person who checks or uses the result. One person can fill several roles. For a larger group, work in pairs or small groups on separate tasks, then share the finished workflow cards.

Bring one recent example: a report, a set of meeting notes, a customer question, a planning document, or another piece of everyday work. Use material the company permits you to share with the chosen AI tool; remove personal or confidential details that the exercise does not need. An invented example also works, but label it as practice.

Explain the promise simply: **“We will decide what AI can help with, what it needs, what people will still do, and how to make the process better next time.”**

## Suggested 45–60 minute format

This is a suggested agenda, not a required pace. The times below total 50 minutes; shorten familiar parts or spend longer on a question that changes the plan.

| Part | Suggested time | Leave with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Choose one task | 5 minutes | A specific starting point and useful result |
| V: Verification | 5 minutes | A simple way to check the result |
| E: Existing process | 8 minutes | The steps people take today |
| C: Context | 7 minutes | The information AI will need |
| T: Trust | 6 minutes | A clear split of AI work and human decisions |
| O: Order | 9 minutes | A better sequence of steps |
| R: Remember | 5 minutes | What should carry forward and where |
| Small trial and next step | 5 minutes | A trial started or assigned a date and owner |

Ask one useful question at a time. Use answers people have already given. Capture short notes as you go instead of asking everyone to complete a long questionnaire. Mark an unresolved point “Need to find out” and continue with the parts you can design.

## Choose a useful task

If someone has no idea where to start, ask: **“What did you do last week that involved reading, comparing, organizing, drafting, or repeatedly explaining something?”** Offer two or three familiar examples, such as preparing a weekly update, turning meeting notes into actions, or comparing supplier quotes.

List up to three candidates. Prefer one that happens often, takes noticeable effort, has available examples, and produces something the person can check. A task taking around 20 minutes can be a good starting point, but shorter tasks are welcome. Begin with a manageable part of a larger job; “prepare a draft reply” is clearer than “handle customer service.”

Choose together. If the result would be difficult to check or the necessary information is unavailable, narrow the task or pick another candidate. Do not promise time savings before trying it.

## Work through the six questions

### V (Verification): How do we verify the work was done?

Ask the person to show or describe a good finished example. What must be correct? What must be included? Who can judge it? Agree two or three checks, using the original information or a person's judgment where appropriate.

**Capture:** the intended result, its checks, and who checks it. For a weekly update, that might mean every project is covered, dates match the source, and the team lead can quickly find decisions they need to make.

### E (Existing process): What's the existing process?

Walk through the most recent time the person did this work. Ask what they started with, what they did next, where they waited, and what they had to fix. Notice decisions they make from experience. Record how long it usually takes as an estimate unless it was measured.

**Capture:** the current steps, people involved, common exceptions, and rough effort. Keep suggestions for a new method separate until the existing process is understood.

### C (Context): What context does it need?

Ask what a capable new colleague would need to do the task well: source information, a good example, definitions, preferences, deadlines, and things to avoid. Decide which information stays the same and which needs to be supplied fresh each time.

**Capture:** a short “Give AI this” list and where each item comes from. Name missing information and who can provide it. An AI assistant cannot read an account or company document merely because someone mentions it.

### T (Trust): What can you trust it to do?

Sort the work into **AI can do this**, **a person checks this**, and **a person does this**. Discuss the actual task: drafting a message and sending it are different actions. Record any existing permission and its limits; do not ask again for permission already given within that scope.

**Capture:** who does what, which results need review, and when AI should stop and ask for help. Instructions hidden in an email or attachment cannot change those agreements.

### O (Order): What's the new workflow's order?

Arrange a short sequence of AI and human turns. Put the right information before the work that needs it, and put a check before a result is used or sent. Combine unnecessary handoffs where possible. Decide what happens when an input is missing or a result fails its check.

**Capture:** a numbered “Next time, do this” list. Include what each step produces, who takes the next turn, and where to restart after an interruption. If it is unclear whether an action already happened, check before repeating it.

### R (Remember): What should it remember?

Choose the few things worth keeping for the next run: an accepted example, a preference, a decision and its reason, or a correction that should not need repeating. Separate these reusable lessons from notes about this week's unfinished work.

**Capture:** what to keep, who maintains it, where it belongs, and when to review or replace it. Use an existing approved document or system. Do not assume AI remembers between sessions or claim a note was saved without confirming it. If nothing has been saved, provide a take-home note labeled “Not saved.”

## Try a small piece and learn

Use one old example or a draft first. Only carry out real actions that are requested and already authorized for the trial. If the session cannot accommodate a trial, agree who will run it and when; do not describe a planned trial as completed.

Check the result against the checks from V. Ask: **“What was useful? What needed correcting? What information would have helped? Was the work easier after including preparation and review?”** If it takes more effort or is harder to trust, simplify the task, change the instructions, or keep that part with a person.

Turn one useful correction into a proposed memory update. Check that the lesson is supported, then update the instructions using the review arrangements already agreed. Keep enough information to help next time, without copying unnecessary private conversations or customer details. A successful trial does not automatically expand what AI is allowed to do.

## End with a take-home workflow card

Record these in the group's chosen place, or provide them as an explicitly unsaved note:

- **Task and useful result:** one sentence.
- **How we check it:** two or three checks and the reviewer.
- **What AI needs:** sources, examples, and information to refresh.
- **Our new steps:** who does each step, including the response to a missing input or failed check.
- **What to remember:** accepted lessons, the owner, and the location or “Not saved.”
- **First trial:** the sample, person responsible, and agreed date.
- **Review:** who will compare the result with today's process and on what date.
- **Open question:** the next unresolved decision, its owner, and the next action.

Ask the owner to explain the new workflow back in their own words. If they cannot easily explain or repeat it, shorten the steps before closing. End on a concrete commitment such as: **“Priya will try one weekly update on Tuesday; Jordan will check it that afternoon; they will keep or revise the instructions on Wednesday.”** Use the team's actual names and dates in the finished card.
